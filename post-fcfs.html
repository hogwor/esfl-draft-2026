Post-Free-Agency Rankings

All fourteen rosters re-priced after Tuesday's free agency, on two seasons of league-scored data rather than one. The earlier version of this page used 2025 alone. That was wrong, it changed several conclusions, and both sets of numbers are shown side by side below.

The correction Why every number on this page moved

The first version of this page priced players off 2025 only. A player who missed most of last season had his short sample regressed toward a generic positional average — so an injury year was treated as evidence of decline rather than as a small sample. That systematically overrated 2025 breakouts and underrated anyone who got hurt.

The fix: blend 2024 and 2025 by games played, discounting 2024 to half weight, and let a short season regress toward the player's own prior year first. 2024 was in the same MFL export the whole time.

It retracts a claim this page made on Sunday. The draft report card said Burrow at 17 was the worst pick in the Western draft and that the room was drafting quarterback reputation over points. Over two seasons Lamar Jackson is the highest-scoring quarterback in this league's data and Burrow is top four. The room was reading two years; this page was reading one. That call was wrong and is withdrawn.

Method

Rosters are the live MFL export from Wednesday 9 September, including every transaction through Tuesday night. Each player's 2025 and 2024 league-scored per-game rates were pulled from the same source. The 2026 projection is a games-weighted blend: rate = (g25·r25 + 0.5·g24·r24 + 3·baseline) / (g25 + 0.5·g24 + 3), multiplied by expected 2026 games. A full-season player carries about 10% baseline weight; a seven-game player carries about 30%. Rookies, retirees and players with no prior line carry a projection instead. Expected games are reduced for documented injury, suspension and age.

Each roster is run through every legal 14-man lineup (QB 1-2 · RB 0-4 · WR 0-4 · TE 1-3 · PK 1 · IDP 5) and the best one is kept. The bar on each card is that lineup's projected points by position, so widths are comparable between teams. Aaron Donald is priced at 160: he is not retired, he is DL1 on the Rams depth chart and listed Out on a coach's decision until 21 September.

Blending two seasons compresses everything. The league spread falls from 982 points on the one-year model to 541 here, which is the honest picture: most of these teams are closer together than a single season's numbers suggested. Treat ±100 on a season total as noise, and treat the eight-team pack as unranked. Every team was run through the same model, including the author's, which lost more to the change than any other roster.

The one-year version of this page ranked Frost first by 298 points and Fields second. On two seasons the lead is 65 and Fields is seventh. Both numbers came from the same model with one parameter changed, which is the most useful thing on this page. Aaron Donald is not retired.
